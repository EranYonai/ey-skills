# The interview

The page is only as good as what the travellers tell you. Ask in short rounds, a few questions at a time, and
build as you go. Never send one giant form.

## How to ask

- **Take whatever they have.** Pasted confirmation emails, PDFs, screenshots, photos of tickets, a messy list, a
  voice-note transcript. Read documents yourself and extract the facts; then play back what you found in a short
  table and ask them to confirm it. People correct a draft far faster than they fill in a blank.
- **Facts only.** Never invent a booking, a time, an address, a phone number, a seat or a reference number, and
  never "tidy" one you cannot read. If a field is missing or illegible, leave it empty, add it to the open items,
  and ask. The page shows "To confirm" for a missing reference, which is honest and useful.
- **Open items are part of the product.** Anything not yet booked or decided becomes a to-do on the page
  (`TODOS`), not a guess.
- **Dates are full ISO dates** (2027-06-05) and **times are local wall-clock times** at the place they happen.
  Confirm the year. Confirm overnight flights ("lands the next day?").
- **One person may be the builder.** If the page is a gift or has surprises, ask early who should know what.
  Collect secrets, letter texts and anything private **from that person only, in a private thread**, and never
  repeat them where the other travellers can read.
- Keep a running list of what is still unknown and show it at the end of every round.

## Round 1: the shape of the trip

- Who is travelling, and what do they call each other? (First names or nicknames exactly as they should appear.
  Any number of people, from one to a big family.)
- Where to, and what is the trip called in their words? ("Greece", "The big road trip".) Is there an occasion
  (honeymoon, anniversary, birthday, graduation)?
- First and last day. Where is home, and which time zone? Which time zone is the destination in?
- **Is this a couple's trip?** If yes, ask whether they want the romantic layer: hearts, a tappable "&" between
  their names, love-letter wording (`romance=True`). For family, friends or solo trips, or if they would rather
  keep it plain, it is off: sparkles instead of hearts and neutral wording. Do not assume from the names.
- Who is asking for the page, and is any of it a surprise for the others?

## Round 2: the bookings

Ask them to forward or upload confirmations. For each:

- **Flights:** airline, flight number, from and to (airport and terminal), date, departure and arrival times,
  whether it lands on another day, booking reference, each passenger's ticket number and seat, baggage allowance.
- **Stays:** name, area, address, phone, room type, board (breakfast?), check-in and check-out dates and times,
  reservation number, anything the confirmation insists on (deposit, passport, pickup to arrange).
- **Boats, trains, buses, booked cars and transfers:** operator, from and to (pier, station), date, times,
  reference, practical notes (be early, what happens in bad weather).
- **Tickets and vouchers as files:** the PDF or a photo of each, to embed in the wallet. Ask which file belongs to
  which booking and which traveller.

## Round 3: the days

- Split the trip into chapters: usually one per place, plus the way there and the way home. Agree a name and a
  short subtitle for each.
- For each day: a short title, fixed times (pickups, check-outs, tours), and loose ideas.
- Places and food they are excited about, per chapter. Whose idea was each one? (Shown as "Sam's pick" and added
  to the shared wishlist.)
- Expected weather per chapter if they know it or you can state it from a reliable source. Otherwise leave it out.

## Round 4: before they go

- Things still to book, decide or do, with any deadline or window ("online check-in opens 24 hours before").
- Packing list, grouped (documents, clothes, tech). Offer a sensible starting list for the destination and let
  them edit it; a packing list is a suggestion, not a fact.
- Contacts worth having: airline, agent, insurance, local emergency number.

## Round 5: handy things

- Currency: destination and home currency, the rate and the day it was looked up (get it from a reliable source
  and say which day; never guess).
- A handful of local phrases and, where the script differs, each stay and station written in the local script
  for a taxi driver. Only include wording you are confident is correct; say so if you are not, and offer to leave
  it out.
- Tips worth knowing: time difference, customs, plugs, tipping, apps.

## Round 6: the look

Show the eight theme pictures (`references/themes.md`) and let them choose. Offer your pick with one reason.
Mention that the stickers on the page can be changed too (sun, plane, suitcase, camera, tram, cactus, peak and
more), and choose a scene type for each chapter with them (`references/scenes-and-art.md`).

## Round 7: surprises (private, with the builder only)

Explain the menu in `references/secrets.md` in two sentences, then ask:

- Which hidden secrets to switch on, and the personal words for each (or use the built-in wording).
- Is there a special date on the trip (a full moon, a birthday, the anniversary itself)? That is the natural day
  for the date-locked moon.
- A number that means something (a date, a house number) for the converter code.
- **Sealed letters written before the trip:** who writes to whom, and on which day each opens. Take the words
  exactly as given; do not polish a love letter unless asked. A letter "from the trip" for the last night is a
  nice default: offer to draft it and let them rewrite it.
- Remind them that travellers can also write sealed notes to each other from their own phones during the trip.

With romance off, the same mechanics work with neutral wording: a birthday surprise, a note to the kids for the
first morning, a letter to everyone for the last night.

## Before building

Read the whole picture back in one message: travellers, dates, each booking in one line, chapters, what is still
open, theme, and (to the builder only) the surprises. Build once they say it is right.
